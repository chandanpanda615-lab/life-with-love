## The honest position on Sarangada and turmeric

**Important.** Sarangada itself is not a major turmeric-producing village. Turmeric is grown in surrounding villages within a 5–10 km radius. This needs to be respected in everything Maati Katha writes — we do not claim to be turmeric producers, and we do not say "our turmeric." We say what is true: we sit inside the registered Kandhamal Haladi production zone, the surrounding villages grow it, and a guest can walk to the fields where it grows.

The framing is that turmeric is a *neighbour* of Sarangada, not a *product* of Sarangada.

---

## What is true and usable

### About the GI tag

- "Kandhamal Haladi" is a Geographical Indication registered with the Government of India
- It is the same kind of protection that covers Champagne, Darjeeling tea, and Roquefort cheese
- It was officially granted on 1 April 2019 (Odisha Foundation Day)
- The registry name is "Haladi" (Odia spelling); newspapers often write "Haldi" (Hindi spelling)

### About the production zone

- The protected area covers twelve tehsils across Kandhamal district
- Sarangada's own block, **K. Nuagaon**, is one of the twelve
- Inside K. Nuagaon block, surrounding villages within 5–10 km of Sarangada grow turmeric — Sarangada itself does not, in any meaningful quantity
- Walking distance from Sarangada will reach turmeric fields. This is something Maati Katha guests can do.

### About the soil and the chemistry

- The same red lateritic soil under Sarangada is the soil under the surrounding turmeric fields
- The protected turmeric carries 3.2 to 4.2 percent curcumin (commercial Indian turmeric averages 2 to 3 percent)
- It is grown without chemical fertiliser or pesticide. Cow dung, sal-leaf mulch, and traditional biological pest control are the standard practice in the district.
- Roughly sixty thousand families across the district grow it — about half the district's population

### About the people who own the GI

- The GI is held by **KASAM** — the Kandhamal Apex Spices Association for Marketing
- KASAM is a producers' marketing association, not a state agency or a private corporation
- It is, in effect, a tribal-cooperative instrument

## Lines safe to use on the website (with light editing)

### Honest version 1 — for the "Land" section

> Sarangada sits inside the registered Kandhamal Haladi zone, one of twelve tehsils whose turmeric carries the protected geographical indication. We do not grow it ourselves in any quantity. The fields begin five kilometres from our doorway, in the next village. The soil there is the same red soil that runs under our houses, and the rain that falls on us falls on those fields the same morning.

### Honest version 2 — for a "Day 4 walk" itinerary description

> On the fourth morning, if the weather is dry, we walk to the neighbouring village where the turmeric is grown. It is a slow walk — the road is not paved past a certain point — and it takes a few hours, with stops. We meet the families who plant it, weed it, and dig it out at the end of the year. Their turmeric carries between three and four percent curcumin, where the turmeric on a city supermarket shelf carries two. It is grown without chemical anything, because the families who grow it never used any — the practice was organic before the word was.

### Honest version 3 — for a "Story of the soil" journal post

> The soil under Sarangada is on a list now. In April 2019, the Government of India registered the turmeric grown across our district as a protected name — the same kind of legal mark that protects Champagne, or Darjeeling tea. The list of permitted villages includes our block, though we are not a major producer. The villages five kilometres from us are. Sixty thousand families across this district grow on the same red soil, in the same monsoon, the way their families did in 1908 when a colonial officer wrote it down for the first time, and the way they did long before he arrived to write it.

## Lines NOT to use

- ❌ "Our turmeric carries the GI tag" — Sarangada doesn't grow it
- ❌ "We grow Kandhamal Haladi" — not true at the village level
- ❌ "Cook with the turmeric we grew on our own farm" — false claim, can damage credibility
- ❌ Any claim that implies Maati Katha *produces* turmeric

If a guest asks where to buy authentic Kandhamal Haladi, the honest answer is: through KASAM's licensed channels (their godown network in five blocks, including K. Nuagaon, with central processing at Bandhagad in Phulbani town).

## The historical quotation worth knowing

The colonial officer L.S.S. O'Malley wrote in 1908:

> *"The growth of turmeric is almost a religious rite with these wild people and it was to improve its colour and output that human sacrifices used to be performed. It is their chief source of income as they export it in large quantities, bartering it for grain and salt..."*

This is the earliest written description of Kandhamal turmeric on the official record. It is also written in colonial language ("these wild people") that frames Kondh practice through the lens of the Meriah suppression campaigns of the 1830s–50s.

For the website: the quotation can be cited carefully, with the colonial framing acknowledged. Do not republish without context. The Meriah question and the colonial archive belong to Layer 3 of the research and need that depth before they appear on the public site.

For now, in any public writing:

- ✅ Acceptable: *"Turmeric has been written about in this district since at least 1908."*
- ❌ Not acceptable: Quoting O'Malley's "wild people" line on the homepage without the surrounding historical and ethical context

## Summary for any future writer pulling from this file

- Sarangada sits inside the GI zone but does not produce turmeric in significant quantity
- The producers are 5–10 km away; this is walkable on Day 4 of a guest stay
- The chemistry, the history, and the cultural context are real and citable
- Never claim Maati Katha grows or sells turmeric
- The turmeric is a thing guests *visit*, not a thing Maati Katha *makes*

This single distinction — neighbour, not producer — is what keeps the brand honest.
