# Brand Identity — Maati Katha

*Master reference. Last updated: 30 April 2026. Lock all decisions here. When something changes, update this file first, then propagate.*

---

## 1. Name

**Maati Katha: The Weight of Belonging**

- **Maati Katha** = the house name. Logo, URL, Instagram handle, signage, business cards, future book spine.
- **The Weight of Belonging** = the editorial line. Subtitle on About page, journal section header, photo essay titles, long-form copy.
- The colon stays. It signals editorial seriousness, not marketing.

### Full identity lockup

> **Maati Katha**
> *The Weight of Belonging*
> A seven-day life-exchange in Sarangada, Kandhamal.
> *No fake experiences. Just life as it is.*

Use the full stack on homepage hero, About page, printed leaflet back. Use lines 1+2 for Instagram bio, business cards, village signage. Use lines 1+3 for SEO page titles.

### What the name means

- *Maati* = soil, but also homeland, your own ground. Pan-Indian, not tribe-specific. No appropriation risk.
- *Katha* = oral story, the told kind, with a teller and a listener. Implies seven days of listening to a place.
- Translates badly on purpose: *the story the soil tells*. Foreign visitors learn the meaning through the website, which is part of the appeal.

## 2. Brand essence

**Two-line manifesto:**

> *No fake experiences. Just life as it is.*
> *We don't host you in a village. The village absorbs you for seven days, and you leave heavier than you came.*

The word "heavier" is doing the work. It is the opposite of what tourism promises (lightness, escape) and matches the emotional weight of the subtitle.

## 3. Color palette

Five colours. Each one is a real thing from Kandhamal, not a Pinterest mood.

| Name | HEX | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Laterite | `#8B3A1F` | Primary anchor color. Wet red soil after first monsoon shower. |
| Sal forest | `#2D3B26` | Deep green, near-black. Sal canopy at noon. Body text and dense surfaces. |
| Turmeric dusk | `#C8842B` | Late golden-hour light on a mud wall. Accent and warmth. |
| Chulha ash | `#E8E1D4` | Warm off-white. Cooled wood-ash, unbleached cotton. The "page" of the brand. |
| Indigo night | `#1F2A3A` | Near-black with blue undertone. Village sky 30 min after sunset. Type and quiet accents. |

**Logic of the palette:** laterite + sal-green is what you literally see standing in the village. Turmeric warms it without making it cheerful. Ash lets the palette breathe. Indigo gives a serious near-black option that isn't pure black (which would feel digital/cold).

**Avoid:** pure white, pure black, saturated blue, any orange that drifts toward festival/spice-route tourism cliché.

## 4. Typography

| Role | Font | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Header | **Cormorant Garamond** (Medium / Semibold) | Slightly old-world, hand-set printed-journal feel. Has weight without polish. Avoid Playfair (overused) and Lora (too soft). |
| Body | **Inter** (Regular, 16–17px, generous line height) | Quiet, neutral, accessible to international readers who can't parse decorative fonts. |
| Accent / journal | **Caveat** (or Homemade Apple) | Single handwritten font. Sparingly — marginalia, photo captions, day-of-week labels. Once per page; more becomes twee. |

Pairing logic: serif header + sans body + occasional handwritten accent = journal-meets-document feel without committing to fully handwritten branding (which would be unreadable at scale).

## 5. Logo direction (locked: Concept B — The Threshold)

**The Doorway with the Rising Sun.**

An ink-sketch line drawing of a single mud-house doorway viewed straight-on — just the lintel, two side posts, a hint of the wooden door slightly ajar. Drawn in a rough, hand-pulled line that varies in weight, like a journal sketch done in five seconds with a fountain pen. Inside the doorway, instead of a dark interior, a tiny rising sun rendered as a half-circle in turmeric-dusk gold. Brand name in Cormorant Garamond sits to the right of the doorway, baseline-aligned with the lintel.

**Why this concept under the Maati Katha name:**
- A doorway is where a *katha* begins — the threshold between outside and inside, where storytelling crosses over.
- Crossing a threshold is also literally what *belonging* requires (guest → held).
- The doorway image holds both halves of the layered name in one mark.
- Geometrically simple → works at small sizes (favicon, embroidery, business card).

**Next step:** generate the SVG via Recraft once we are ready to commit visually.

## 6. Hero section — current direction

Split layout:
- **Left half (cream paper / chulha ash):** the journal register. Wordmark, italic Caveat handwritten line ("a seven-day life-exchange in Kandhamal, Odisha"), large Cormorant headline ("The Weight of Belonging."), body paragraph, CTA, tagline as quiet line under CTA.
- **Right half (sal forest green):** the night/chulha register. Footprint or doorway sketch in laterite, coordinates in Cormorant, italic guest testimonial in Cormorant, small Caveat note ("no wifi after 7pm. nobody minds.").

The brand lives in the tension between the two surfaces. Most travel sites pick one mood; Maati Katha needs both — the cream is the *journal*, the green is the *night*.

## 7. Editorial voice

- Slow, observational, present-tense.
- First-person plural when referring to the village ("we", "our soil").
- Second-person singular when addressing the visitor ("you will wake").
- Undramatic. If something is ordinary, call it ordinary.
- No travel-brochure language: avoid "vibrant", "exotic", "unspoiled", "hidden gem", "off the beaten path", "authentic experience".
- No romanticization of poverty. The rust, the bare earth, the worn building are presented plainly.
- Willing to make small jokes at its own expense (prevents tipping into solemn-NGO territory).
- Engages honestly with contested history (Kandhamal 2008, Meriah-era colonial archive). Does not sanitize.

## 9. What changes vs what stays

When this brand grows beyond Sarangada (potentially a future *Maati Katha — Bastar* or *— Spiti*):

**Stays the same:** the name, the tagline, the palette, the typography, the editorial voice, the seven-day format.

**Changes:** the village name in the descriptor line, the specific geography of the hero, the local foodways and rituals, the testimonials, the journal entries.

The brand is built as a vessel, not a single location. *Maati Katha* names the format; *Sarangada* names this first instance.
